OGO Energy, R&D Engineer

Noida, India · Apr to Jul 2026 · battery storage start-up

I worked on the electronics that keep home and telecom batteries safe (the battery management system), across testing, partner integration and documentation.

Cut quality-check time per unit by about 80%

Problem
Every battery control board went through a 9-step check before shipping, mostly by hand. One unit took 30 to 45 minutes, so testing capped how many units could ship.
What I did
Kept the few checks that need a person (switch voltages, communication, fan, status lights) and moved the rest into an automated test app.
Result
5 to 10 minutes per unit. The same bench can now clear roughly five times as many boards a shift.

Made our battery work with a partner's inverter

Problem
To sell alongside Solis inverters, our battery had to report the right limits and alarms to the inverter and its mobile app. Solis reviewed our joint test report and raised 14 comments.
What I did
Checked that charge limits, state of charge, voltage, current and alarms matched across the battery, the inverter screen and the app, then coordinated the extra tests and documentation each comment needed.
Result
All 14 comments closed. I also flagged that the inverter's recommended charging setting used only part of what the battery can take.

Charging current, amps

The same checks covered charge and discharge limits, state of charge, voltage, current, alarms and fault handling.

Investigated a failure at a customer site

Problem
A battery system at a customer's solar storage site stopped powering on and went silent on its data link. The customer needed an answer and a plan to stop it happening again.
What I did
Worked with the site engineer on what happened on site, examined the returned board part by part, and traced the damage back to a single insulation failure that took out four components in a chain.
Result
A formal 11-section failure report and a tracker for every future return, so repeat patterns show up early.

Tata Motors, Project Trainee, Advanced Quality

Jamshedpur, India · Jan to May 2023

Fewer faulty fuel sensors, fewer warranty claims

Problem
Fuel-level sensors in trucks were failing often enough to show up in the warranty quality metric (incidents per thousand vehicles). Water was getting into the electronics and the old sealing was not working.
What I did
Visited warranty garages, logged faults on 100+ sensors and ranked the causes. Then worked with quality, design and the supplier on low-cost fixes. I also found an outside sensor product and pitched it to the Deputy General Manager, who agreed to explore it.
Result
About 15% better reliability. The new design sat underwater for six months with zero errors.

M.Tech thesis: two motors that share the work

RGIPT · 2024 to 2025 · ranked 1st in batch

Getting more out of every charge

Problem
A single electric motor wastes energy at many speeds, and range is one of the biggest worries for electric car buyers.
What I did
Designed the control logic for a car with a motor on each axle. It decides, moment by moment, how much work each motor does, while keeping the battery inside safe limits. Tested in simulation on the Indian urban driving cycle (MATLAB, GT-SUITE).
Result
The car followed its target speed within 2 km/h through stop-and-go traffic, both motors stayed mostly in their best efficiency zone, and braking energy went back into the battery.
